About the model

A plain-language look at how DermaScan works, what it can and can't do, and how your photo is handled. No technical background needed.

⚠ Not a medical device, not validated for clinical use. DermaScan is an educational project and is not a substitute for professional medical advice.

What DermaScan does

DermaScan looks at a photo of skin and estimates how severe the eczema appears, giving a simple "high" or "low" reading. It does not diagnose eczema and it does not replace a doctor, only think of it as a rough, second-opinion estimate, not a verdict.

How it works

DermaScan uses two AI models computer programs that learn patterns from examples rather than following fixed rules. Each one looks at your image on its own and gives its own opinion:

  • One is a custom model built from scratch specifically for spotting eczema severity.
  • The other is a model that was first trained on millions of everyday photos, then adapted to recognise skin conditions. Starting from broad visual knowledge often helps it learn from a small dataset.

We show you both opinions side by side, so you can see when the two models agree (more reassuring) or disagree (a sign to be more cautious).

What the heatmap shows (Grad-CAM)

Alongside the result you'll see a colourful "heatmap" laid over your photo. It shows which parts of the image the model paid the most attention to when making its decision:

  • Warm colours (red, orange) mark the areas the model focused on most.
  • Cool colours (blue, green) mark the areas it paid less attention to.

This is a quick way to sanity-check the result: if the warm colours sit on the affected skin, the model is looking in the right place. If they sit on the background or clothing instead, treat the result with extra caution.

What this is not

  • Not a medical diagnostic tool.
  • Not a replacement for seeing a dermatologist.
  • It cannot tell you what type of skin condition you have.
  • It may give a confident-sounding answer even for photos that aren't of skin at all.
  • It was trained on a small dataset, so its accuracy is limited.
  • It has not been validated for clinical use.

About the training data

  • It learned from roughly 199 labelled eczema images.
  • Each image was marked as either "high" or "low" severity by the people who originally put the dataset together.
  • That's a small dataset, which directly limits how reliable the model can be.
  • No demographic balancing was applied, so how well it performs across different skin tones is unverified.

Your privacy

  • Your photo is used only for the moment of analysis and never leaves it.
  • Nothing is saved, stored, or shared.
  • We don't track who uses DermaScan or what images they upload.
